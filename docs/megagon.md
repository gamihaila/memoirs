
After we got back from Switzerland, at the end of summer in 2016, our
team went out to celebrate our recent launch of the improved question
answering system at Cascal, an upscale Spanish cuisine restaurant
downtown Mountain View. We got seated at a long table in the back of
the restaurant. After we finished, I thought I heard a familiar voice
and turned around: it was Alon Halevy, who had recently left Google to
take on a CEO role at a new research lab opened in Mountain View by
Recruit Holdings, a large Japanese conglomerate. He was having lunch
with his new team. Naturally, we exchanged a few words about his new
venture, and he said "Let's keep in touch!". I knew what that meant.
I waited for my Google teammates to leave and I said, discreetly:
"Sure, you know where to find me."

And sure enough, after a few days I got an e-mail from him, asking me
if I was available for a chat over lunch. We set a time and place for
the following week, and we met. There, Alon told me all about the kind
of work they were doing at the new lab, and how the Japanese parent
company had given him complete freedom to grow the team over the next
few years. "If you're interested, we'd love to invite you to meet the
team.". So I first went there for an informal chat with some of the
senior researchers who told me about the work they were doing pushing
the boundaries of natural language understanding and building chatbot
prototypes for various application domains (this was years before
anybody heard of ChatGPT). I was hooked!

After a few days I wrote to Alon that I'd like to go forward, so his
office manager booked a proper interview schedule for the following
week. I again solved a bunch of design and coding questions on the
whiteboard, and also had some more discussions with the
researchers. The role I was interviewing for was called "research engineer"
and they were specifically looking for someone that can connect the
researchers with the engineers. "That's what I have been doing for the
past 16 years at IBM and Google", I said.

After a couple more weeks, Alon called me on my cellphone:

"Can you talk?", he opened.

"Let me step outside", I said, because I was at work.

"OK, here is the deal: everybody in the team is very excited, we'd
love for you to join us. What is your current level at Google?"

"I'm a Level 4 software engineer"

"OK, I know from my own experience that leaving Google is not an easy
decision, but if you join, we'll promote you to Level 5 and raise your
compensation by 40%. Interested?

"Thank you, Alon! I'll give you my answer before the end of the
week.", I said.
